HOUSE

THE ROOTS

House took shape in Chicago’s club scene in the early 1980s. DJs brought together disco, soul and electronic sounds, while producers explored drum machines and synthesizers. Clubs such as the Warehouse, where Frankie Knuckles played, became an important part of its story.

THE SOUND

Listen for a steady kick on every beat, dancing hi-hats and a repeating bassline. Piano chords, soulful vocals and looping rhythms often add warmth. House has many different styles, but the groove keeps pulling you forward.

THE CONNECTION

House grew in spaces shaped by Black, Latino and LGBTQ+ communities. The dancefloor offered room for expression and belonging. Its influence travelled far beyond Chicago, connecting dancers through a shared rhythm.

TECHNO

THE ROOTS

Techno took shape in Detroit during the 1980s. Juan Atkins, Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson became central figures in its development. Drawing on electronic music and funk, they explored new sounds through synthesizers, drum machines and a futuristic imagination.

THE SOUND

A steady kick, repeating patterns and electronic textures often drive the music. Listen closely: a filter opens, a hi-hat enters, a bassline changes. Small shifts can transform the atmosphere. Techno can be deep and melodic, raw and minimal, or intense and fast.

THE CONNECTION

Rooted in Black musical innovation in Detroit, techno travelled into club scenes around the world. Cities such as Berlin helped shape new directions. Across different scenes, the dancefloor became a place to explore sound, movement and collective experience.

ELECTRO

THE ROOTS

Electro emerged in the early 1980s, bringing together funk, electronic music and hip-hop. Afrika Bambaataa and the Soulsonic Force’s “Planet Rock” became a landmark recording, drawing on the electronic sounds of Kraftwerk. Drum machines helped give this music its distinctive pulse.

THE SOUND

Listen for syncopated drum patterns, sharp snares and electronic basslines. Unlike the steady kick often heard in house, electro frequently uses a broken rhythm. Vocoders can turn voices into robotic textures, while synthesizers bring the futuristic atmosphere.

THE CONNECTION

Early electro became closely connected with hip-hop and breaking. Its rhythms travelled through clubs, street dance and DJ culture. Later artists developed new directions, from playful funk to darker machine music. Electro is a distinct tradition, not simply another name for electro house.

DRUM AND BASS

THE ROOTS

Drum & bass developed in the UK during the 1990s, emerging from jungle and the breakbeat rave scene. Its roots connect electronic production with hip-hop, reggae, dub and Caribbean sound-system culture.

THE SOUND

Fast breakbeats and deep bass drive the sound. Listen for chopped drum samples, rolling rhythms and the contrast between rapid drums and slower bass movement. Styles range from melodic liquid to darker, more intense sounds.

THE CONNECTION

Raves, pirate radio and sound systems helped build the scene. DJs and MCs created an exchange with the crowd, making energy something shared. From its UK roots, drum & bass grew into an international community.

AMBIENT

THE ROOTS

Ambient draws on experimental music, minimalism and electronic sound. Brian Eno helped define the term during the 1970s, exploring music that could support different levels of attention.

THE SOUND

Listen for sustained tones, spacious textures and gradual changes. Synthesizers, acoustic instruments and field recordings can all become part of the landscape. A dance beat is not the central focus.

THE CONNECTION

Ambient invites you to slow down and notice the space around sound. It can accompany quiet listening, installations or chill-out rooms. Here, it becomes a moment of rest after the energy of the dancefloor.
